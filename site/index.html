The current thesis

Most compliance regimes quietly push companies toward automating the human out of a process — and that's backwards. Automate a regulated process fully, and it inherits the whole certification burden. Keep a human accountable and let AI only augment them, and that burden doesn't transfer. The organizations that understand this will out-adopt everyone else, safely.

— on the Compliance–Innovation Paradox, one of four papers in the current research agenda

Working papers · design science research

Research agenda — four papers in preparation

  • 01Vibe Coding in Regulated Industry: A Governed Experimentation Framework. A two-track governance model — sandboxed experimentation vs. hardened promotion — with an explicit promotion gate and a threshold model for when human review costs more than regeneration. Target: ICIS / ECIS · Business & Information Systems Engineering
  • 02The Compliance–Innovation Paradox: Augmentation vs. Automation under Regulated AI Use. Testing the claim that regulatory load is a forcing function toward augmentation, not a brake on AI adoption. Target: Academy of Management Discoveries / Technovation
  • 03From Crowd to Lab to Audit: An Institutional Model of AI Learning. Extends the Leadership–Crowd–Lab model with a knowledge-retention loop that survives staff turnover and satisfies an auditor. Target: Journal of Management Information Systems / CIRP CMS
  • 04Vibe Coding and the SME: Democratized Innovation with Guardrails. Adapting the framework for small-enterprise constraints — no IT department, no internal auditor, owner-operator accountability. Target: Journal of Small Business Management
